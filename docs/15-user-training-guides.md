# User Training Guides

Training is part of support. A user who learns a task once creates fewer tickets later. This page covers a simple method for one-on-one training and includes ready-to-use quick guides.

## 1. A simple method for one-on-one training

1. **Ask first.** What does the person already know, and what do they need to do?
2. **Set one goal.** Teach one task at a time.
3. **Show once.** Demonstrate at a pace they can follow, saying what you click and why.
4. **Let them do it.** The user performs the steps while you watch. Do not take the mouse.
5. **Check.** Ask them to explain it back or repeat it without help.
6. **Leave a guide.** Give a one-page quick guide with screenshots, and say where to get help.
7. **Follow up.** Check in after a few days and record the questions asked.

## 2. Principles

- Use plain language and avoid jargon. Explain the first time you use a term.
- Be patient. Never make someone feel foolish.
- Prefer short sessions (15 to 20 minutes) and practice on real work.
- Provide written steps in the order the person will do them.
- Respect privacy. Do not read the user's documents.

## 3. Quick guide: sign in with multi-factor authentication

1. Open the work application and enter your work email address.
2. Enter your password.
3. Approve the sign-in in the Authenticator app, or enter the code.
4. If you did not start this sign-in, choose Deny and call the service desk.
5. If you get a new phone, contact the service desk before you reset the old one.

## 4. Quick guide: connect to a shared printer

1. Open Settings, then Printers & scanners.
2. Choose Add device and wait for the list.
3. Select the printer that matches the label on the printer (name or location).
4. Print a test page.
5. If the printer is not on the list, contact the service desk with the printer label.

## 5. Quick guide: join a Teams meeting

1. Open the meeting in your calendar and select Join.
2. Before you join, check that the correct microphone, speaker and camera are selected.
3. Select Join now, and mute yourself when you are not speaking.
4. To share your screen, select Share and choose the window or screen.
5. If you cannot be heard, check the mute button and the device setting, then use the Test call option in Settings.

## 6. Quick guide: save files so they are backed up

1. Save work to OneDrive or the department's network drive. Avoid saving only on the computer's Desktop or Downloads unless they are synced.
2. Check the OneDrive cloud icon: a green check means the file is synced.
3. To open a file from another device, sign in at office.com and open OneDrive.

## 7. Quick guide: spot a phishing email

1. Check the sender's real address, not just the display name.
2. Look for urgency, unexpected attachments, requests for passwords or gift cards.
3. Hover over links to see where they go. Do not click if unsure.
4. Report the message using the Report button or forward it to the security team.
5. If you clicked or entered a password, call the service desk immediately.

## 8. Group training sessions

Plan the agenda, test the room and the equipment, bring printed quick guides, and leave time for questions. Share slides afterward and record the common questions for the knowledge base.

## 9. Measuring whether training worked

Fewer repeat tickets on the same topic, user confidence in a follow-up question, and the time it takes users to complete the task unaided.

# Deskside and Field Service Visit Checklist

Deskside, walk-up and field service work is face-to-face. Technical skill is half of it. The other half is preparation, communication, safety and documentation.

## 1. Before leaving the desk

- [ ] Read the whole ticket: symptom, location, contact, access instructions, deadline
- [ ] Contact the user to confirm availability and location (building, floor, desk, room number)
- [ ] Check for related tickets and the device's history in the asset system
- [ ] Prepare the tools and parts you may need (see the kit list below)
- [ ] Download any required drivers, installers and documentation ahead of time, or save them offline for sites without good connectivity
- [ ] For field sites: confirm site access, escort or badge, parking, working hours, and a local contact
- [ ] Tell the service desk where you are going and your expected return time
- [ ] Charge your phone and laptop

## 2. Toolkit

| Category | Items |
| --- | --- |
| Software | Bootable USB with recovery and diagnostic tools, approved installers, driver packs, remote support tool, password manager access, offline KB |
| Cables and adapters | Ethernet (several lengths), USB-A/USB-C, HDMI, DisplayPort, USB-C to HDMI/DisplayPort/Ethernet adapters, power strips |
| Hand tools | Precision screwdriver set, spudger, tweezers, flashlight, cable tester, cable ties, labeler |
| Spares | Chargers, mice, keyboards, headsets, docks, small stock of RAM and SSDs where policy allows, loaner laptop |
| Diagnostics | Network cable tester, Wi-Fi analyzer app, multimeter (for advanced field work), thermal paste and cleaning supplies as needed |
| Safety and admin | Anti-static strap, gloves, hand truck for heavy equipment, ID badge, asset labels, equipment acknowledgment forms |

## 3. Arriving on site

1. Introduce yourself, confirm the user's name and the ticket number.
2. Verify the user's identity if you will touch accounts or take over a managed device.
3. Ask the user to show the problem. Re-ask the triage questions: when did it begin, what changed.
4. Ask about unsaved work and warn before restarting or reimaging.
5. Protect confidentiality. Do not read documents, emails or screens beyond what you need.

## 4. Doing the work

- Back up or confirm data is synced before any destructive step.
- Change one thing at a time and test after each step.
- Swap known good parts to isolate faults: cable, port, power adapter, monitor, dock.
- Follow ESD safety when opening devices. Power off, unplug and remove the battery when the model requires it.
- Follow the lock-out rules for equipment you do not own, and do not work in telecom closets or server rooms without authorization.
- Lift with proper technique. Use a cart for heavy items, and never leave cables across walkways.
- Keep the area clean. Take trash and packaging with you.

## 5. Common deskside tasks

**New laptop handover**: sign in, confirm MFA, check apps, printers, VPN, Teams audio, OneDrive sync, a docking station and monitors, give a short orientation.

**Docking station and monitor setup**: ensure a supported cable and port, update dock firmware, set the display layout and refresh rate, and tidy cables.

**Peripheral swap**: record the old item's asset tag, label and register the new item, and return the old item to stock or disposal.

**Device refresh and data migration**: confirm OneDrive backup of Desktop, Documents and Pictures, browser profiles, printers, mapped drives, and application licenses, then assign the new device and retire the old.

**VIP or executive support**: the same security procedures apply. Aim for fast, discreet, proactive service. Confirm the preferred contact method, reduce interruption, and follow up the same day.

**Walk-up support**: post hours, use a queue or sign-in sheet, create a ticket for every visit even for quick fixes (it proves demand and is useful for reporting), and set a time limit before moving to a scheduled ticket.

## 6. Field service specifics

- Document the site layout, network closets, ISP demarcation and the contact names.
- Label everything you install (device, cable ends, ports).
- Check power, cooling and physical security of equipment.
- Perform acceptance tests: connectivity, speed test, printing, sign-in, application access.
- For network gear, record the model, serial number, firmware and ports, and take photos before and after.
- Never leave default or shared passwords on devices. Record credentials only in the approved password vault.
- Unplanned travel needs approval, and mileage and time tracking according to policy.

## 7. Safety

- Wear appropriate shoes and PPE for the site.
- Do not work on energized power equipment unless qualified.
- Be careful with lithium-ion batteries: a swollen battery must not be charged or punctured. Isolate it, tell the user and follow the hazardous-waste procedure.
- Report injuries and hazards immediately.

## 8. Wrap-up

- [ ] Test the fix together with the user and confirm they are satisfied
- [ ] Clean the work area, remove tools, packaging and old parts
- [ ] Give the user a clear summary and how to reach you if it returns
- [ ] Update the ticket right away: cause, steps, parts used, time on site, follow-ups
- [ ] Update the asset record (device swapped, location, owner)
- [ ] Return loaners, restock the kit, report low inventory
- [ ] Add a KB article if the issue is likely to recur

## 9. Soft skills that matter

Listen first. Explain what you are doing in plain language. Stay calm with frustrated users. Be punctual and tell people early if you will be late. Own the follow-up. Never promise what you cannot deliver.
